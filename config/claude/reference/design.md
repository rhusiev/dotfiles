# Product interface design

design_rules:
  ABOUT:
    - "How to design a product's interface with me - an app, a site, a tool, any
       medium. This is the method and the general style I want everywhere. A
       product's own values - colors, names, screens, decisions - live in its guide
       or its docs, never here. The PHONE group adds the rules for phone apps"

  MUST:
    - "Design before building. Settle the scope, the visual direction and the screens
       before implementation starts, and keep design work ahead of the build. Do not
       start an implementation step while design review is still open"
    - "Ask the opening questions as one numbered list, ordered by how much each
       answer changes the design. Give each a short explanation and a default, so I
       can answer 'default'"
    - "Research before drawing: inventory the features of comparable products, open
       and proprietary, with confirmed versus inferred evidence. Propose v1, v1.x,
       later or never per feature and escalate only the real calls. Every deferred
       feature gets a backlog entry with its date and origin, and leaves it only by
       landing or by an explicit, dated drop"
    - "Check the license of anything that inspires a design. Reuse its rules, assets
       or names only when their license is compatible with ours. Otherwise take the
       ideas, write our own and credit the inspiration"
    - "You make the prototypes; I narrow them down. Round 1 is several genuinely
       different directions, not variations of one idea. Round 2 combines the parts I
       liked into named hybrids. Then refine the winner into written layout rules
       and apply them to every screen"
    - "Narrow a brand or icon the same way: several directions, shown at real sizes
       and in context. Keep the survivors, each with its one named risk, and make
       variations aimed at that risk. Archive rejected directions instead of
       deleting them"
    - "Build prototypes in the medium closest to the real product, at its real size,
       generated by scripts from shared components and constants, with plausible
       fake data. Every round reuses the same components, so the language stays one"
    - "Keep one written design language for the whole product, in the product's own
       docs (for example docs/DESIGN.md), not in a session file. It covers tokens,
       type, spacing, icons, every control and its states, hints, copy rules and the
       shared components, including those that plugin cards and extensions reuse.
       Every screen, plugin card and round uses it. Add a new pattern to it before
       the first screen uses it, and check each screen against it before review"
    - "Draw only what the product can provide. Read the product docs and the code
       contracts before you draw a value, a state or a setting. Invent no feature,
       number or option. Mark estimates and examples as such. When you must decide a
       product detail to draw a screen, list it separately for my review"
    - "Review each round on one clean page that I can reach, with only what is up
       for review: the screens, the choices with a default and an alternative, what
       is still open, the proposed copy in every language the product uses, and the
       consequences for the implementation. Put the choices on the page, not only in
       chat. Stop servers that serve nothing to review"
    - "Each time the review page is updated, show on it only the screenshots that show
       what changed or what is up for a decision. Move every other screenshot to an
       archive section or page"
    - "Record each decision when I make it, turn the round's choices into a Decided
       list, and keep a later round authoritative over earlier ones. When a later
       round changes an element, list the outdated screens that still show it"
    - "Settle choices that cost performance or quality by measuring throwaway
       prototypes, one lever at a time, with side-by-side renders before I accept
       any lossy change"
    - "When a sub-agent builds screens, give it the design language, the layout rules
       and 'invent nothing'. Then review every screen yourself at full size against
       the canon before I see it: layout, drift from the tokens, invented data,
       outdated names, contrast. Its report is a lead"
    - "Keep my design critiques verbatim, with dates, in the product's design notes.
       Before a round reaches me, review every screen against them and against the
       STYLE rules, and fix what they would flag. For a large round, add an
       independent reviewer that uses the same critiques"

  STYLE:
    - "Arrange by use: what is read sits where the eye goes first, and what is
       pressed often sits where it is cheapest to reach on the target device. Break
       this only with my approval; a small control may stay next to the one element
       it changes"
    - "Simple by default, powerful when needed: advanced options sit one step away,
       not inline"
    - "Make the scope of every change visible: whether it changes a default or only
       the current item, and how to go back"
    - "Be transparent about data: every action that sends data elsewhere says what
       it sends and to whom, next to the control that sends it. Choose the
       privacy-conscious default"
    - "Give color a job and validate it: check every categorical or diverging
       palette for color-blind readers and contrast in every theme, with a
       validator, never by eye. Use a texture when no color passes. A shared hue
       means a related thing"
    - "Design a dark theme screen by screen from its own tokens. Never invert the
       light one"
    - "Copy is short and comes from one glossary term per concept. US English by
       default, British English and my other languages beside it, every string
       listed with its translations. A distinctive name gets a one-line hint"
    - "Quiet lists: no separator lines where spacing reads well"
    - "Each fact once. Cut copy that repeats what a status, chart, table, heading or
       nearby control already shows. A heading or column label carries shared context,
       so values drop it: '0 %' under 'Loss', not '0 % loss'"
    - "Short labels, with a '?' hint for the meaning instead of sentences on the page.
       Replace jargon with plain words, or hint it. A hint sits right next to its
       label, opens on hover and keyboard focus, and fits without scrolling"
    - "Numbers at the precision a decision needs, usually two decimals at most, always
       with units. Lead with the number that matters most, such as a loss percentage
       rather than a lost count. A derived metric says what it measures, which
       direction is better and its scale. A limit value reads as a word, such as
       'identical', never as infinity"
    - "Act on the thing itself: a name, number or icon is the link or control. No
       separate text button such as 'Open Trial 2' or 'Refresh log'"
    - "Every control comes from the design language. No raw native checkboxes or
       selects where the language has a switch or a menu"
    - "One object, one view: show a packet, a run or a trial with the same component
       everywhere, including inside plugin cards, which add only their own section"
    - "Hover popups and tooltips float above the page and never move other content"
    - "Charts are interactive where possible: hover details in the shared popup style,
       targets that stay easy to hit when sparse or zoomed, a way to reset zoom, and a
       link from a mark to its detail"
    - "Large sections go into tabs, not accordions"
    - "Overlays on pictures and charts keep their contrast on any content, including
       noisy or saturated images"
    - "Screens must look designed, not assembled by a developer: one grid, one spacing
       scale, aligned edges, a clear hierarchy and the language's components"

  PHONE:
    - "Prototype phone screens at a fixed phone viewport, rendered to PNG at 2x"
    - "Read at the top, press at the bottom: status and read-only information at the
       top, every frequent control within one-thumb reach at the bottom. Exceptions
       only with my approval, such as a back chevron in a list header. Count the
       controls outside thumb reach per screen"

  SHOULD:
    - "When I dislike a pattern but cannot name a better one, show two or three
       alternatives side by side on the review page"
    - "Prefer the industry-standard pattern when it fits, and name it"
    - "When someone else builds what you designed, hand the design over in one
       document: the precedence rule, the decisions, the outdated elements with
       their replacements, the implied code and doc changes, and the known gaps"
