# Simplified Technical English

Use these rules for chat, project documentation and local guides. GENERAL takes precedence, as does EXPLAINING_TO_ME in chat. Code comments follow code style. Commit messages, issues, MR/PR descriptions and thread replies follow the Git and review rules instead

## Write for one reading

- Use active voice unless the actor is unknown or irrelevant. Keep the subject, verb and article explicit. Give one instruction per sentence. Use at most 20 words for procedures and 25 for descriptions
- Use at most three consecutive nouns. Keep a paragraph to one topic and six sentences. Use a list for three or more steps or conditions. Split clauses instead of joining them with a semicolon
- Prefer simple tenses. Keep and flag a compound tense when it conveys current relevance or uncertainty that a simple tense would lose. "The job may have failed" does not mean "the job failed"
- Use one term for each concept. Define unfamiliar technical terms on first use. Prefer a direct verb to a noun phrase: "analyze" rather than "perform an analysis". Replace phrasal verbs: use "start" rather than "spin up"
- Use one part of speech per word where practical. Lexical choices are guidance, not a claim of compliance with ASD's official dictionary
- Check for changing names, stacked qualifiers, actions hidden in nouns, marketing adjectives, run-on sentences and vague phrasal verbs. Remove unsupported claims, not genuine uncertainty

## When rewriting

Read the whole passage for meaning first. Check each sentence against the rules, then revise. Preserve facts, numbers, safety conditions, scope, exceptions and confidence. Do not invent a cause or frequency. If shortening loses precision, keep the longer wording and flag the trade-off rather than silently changing the meaning

Return the rewritten text alone unless asked for the analysis. Do not reproduce the official dictionary or apply this process to creative, persuasive or marketing copy where voice is the point
